# Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview

This project presents an Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) of employee attrition data.

The analysis focuses on understanding employee attrition patterns across departments and job roles, along with workplace factors such as overtime, job satisfaction, monthly income and years at the company.

## Objectives

- Analyze the overall employee attrition rate
- Compare attrition across departments
- Identify job roles with different attrition levels
- Examine the relationship between overtime and attrition
- Analyze job satisfaction and attrition
- Compare monthly income between employees who stayed and left
- Analyze attrition across different tenure groups
- Generate meaningful HR-related insights from the data

## Dataset

**Dataset:** IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance

**Source:** Kaggle

The dataset contains employee-related information including department, job role, job satisfaction, overtime, monthly income, years at company and attrition status.

The raw dataset is not included in this repository.

## Key Insights

- The overall employee attrition rate in the dataset is approximately 16.12%.
- Attrition varies across departments, with Sales showing the highest department-level attrition rate.
- Sales Representatives have the highest attrition rate among the analyzed job roles.
- Employees working overtime show a higher attrition rate than employees who do not work overtime.
- Lower job satisfaction levels are associated with higher observed attrition rates in the dataset.
- Employees who left have a lower average monthly income than employees who stayed.
- Employees with 0–2 years at the company show the highest attrition rate among the analyzed tenure groups.
